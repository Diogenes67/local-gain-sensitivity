## Methods

### Hook-based spectral estimation

For a target block l, a forward pre-hook captures the block's input activations on a natural forward pass and every keyword argument the block receives (attention masks, position embeddings, cache state), and the block is then called directly as a function of one token's hidden state. The local function f: R^d^ → R^d^ maps the hidden state of token t before the block to the hidden state of the same token after it, with all other positions held fixed at their natural values; J = ∂h~l+1,t~/∂h~l,t~ is therefore a d × d matrix and σ~1~(J) is its leading singular value. Each iteration is v ← J^T^(Jv)/‖J^T^(Jv)‖, with the forward product Jv computed by torch.func.jvp (forward-mode automatic differentiation) and the adjoint product J^T^w by reverse-mode autograd on the same hooked call; σ~1~ is read as ‖Jv‖ at convergence, when the relative change falls below 10^−6^. No Jacobian is materialised and no gradient is propagated through the rest of the network. Each estimate uses 50 power-iteration steps from four random starts, batched, with the maximum over starts reported; the value at a block is the mean over five inputs and eight token positions spaced from T/8 to T − 1 (40 samples per block), and the per-sample values are retained for the bootstrap. TF32 matrix multiplication was disabled and all models were loaded with eager attention, because scaled dot-product attention kernels do not support forward-mode differentiation. For convolutional models the token is a spatial position of the feature map and the eight positions form an interior grid. Mamba models used the reference PyTorch selective-scan implementation, which forward-mode differentiation handles exactly. One script (survey_sigma1_v2.py) produced every pretrained-model σ~1~ value in this paper; its plumbing test reproduces the singular value decomposition of materialised Jacobians to a relative error of about 10^−7^ on small randomly initialised instances of all 17 architectures profiled.

Every profile in this paper is a token-local *J*^T^*J* power iteration in float32; the implementation differs in three places, each noted in Extended Data Table 3 and in its legend. The width × depth grid (Extended Data Fig. 9b) and the training trajectories of Extended Data Fig. 4a,b were profiled by the scripts that trained them, at the single position T/2, and the March 2026 factorial shown for the record in Extended Data Fig. 5c by its training script's iteration at one position on random token ids; the factorial, destroyed-data, switching, routing-topology, skip-coefficient and k-gram models were profiled by survey_sigma1_v2 itself (causal mask, five inputs × eight positions, four restarts), and Extended Data Table 3 records the operator, Jacobian domain, intervention, precision and script for every result. Some experiments were first run in March or May 2026 under a different estimator and have since been re-measured or superseded; every such case, and the class of evidence each result now rests on, is set out in Supplementary Note 5, so that the authoritative run for any number is identifiable from one place.

### Estimator validation

The token-local Jacobian was materialised with torch.func.jacrev and decomposed exactly at five blocks spread over the depth, three positions and two natural inputs, in GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, Pythia-410M and Phi-2 (WikiText-103 inputs, positions T/8, T·0.56 and T − 1; 120 pairs, d between 768 and 2,560), and under the same protocol in ConvNeXt-tiny, ConvNeXt-base and Mamba-130M (CIFAR-100 and WikiText-103 inputs; 90 pairs; d = 96–1,024 for ConvNeXt, where the token is a spatial position and d the channel count, and 768 for Mamba). For each the exact σ~1~, σ~1~(J − I) and spectral radius ρ (largest eigenvalue modulus of the dense Jacobian) were compared with the J^T^J power iteration, with an iteration on J, and with the maximum gain over 64 random unit directions. The values reported are from a rerun of this validation on 25 September 2026 (survey_sigma1_v2 v2.3, --validate, A100-40GB) with every per-pair value written to persistent storage; the original runs of 16 and 18 September survive as three-decimal printed logs, which the rerun reproduces pair by pair to a maximum relative difference of 1.9 × 10^−4^, the precision of the logs (Supplementary Note 5). Relative error of the σ~1~ estimate: transformers median 7.5 × 10^−5^, maximum 3.0 × 10^−4^; ConvNeXt-tiny median 1.8 × 10^−5^, maximum 9.4 × 10^−5^; ConvNeXt-base 2.3 × 10^−5^ and 1.2 × 10^−4^; Mamba-130M 5.3 × 10^−5^ and 1.2 × 10^−4^; Pearson r = 1.000 in every model. Of the ρ estimate: median 2.9 × 10^−3^, maximum 0.21 (transformers), 0.08–0.11 (ConvNeXt), 0.09 (Mamba). Random probes returned 8–38% of σ~1~ in the transformers and 12–56% in ConvNeXt and Mamba. σ~1~/ρ ranged 1.5–11.3 in the transformers, 1.5–26 in ConvNeXt-tiny, 1.6–11.3 in ConvNeXt-base and 1.6–8.9 in Mamba-130M. Convergence to tolerance 10^−6^ took a median of 16 iterations over the 210 pairs (per-model medians 10–21; 12 pairs reached the cap of 50, all with relative error below 3.0 × 10^−4^). For Mamba-130M, the dense Jacobian of the full recurrent scan was materialised at sequence lengths 4 and 8 (3,072 × 3,072 and 6,144 × 6,144) for layers 0, 12 and 23 and five inputs of uniformly random token ids each (30 pairs; rerun on 25 September 2026 with the same design as the original run, whose result files were not retained). The J^T^J power iteration agrees with exact decomposition of this whole-sequence operator to a median relative error of 2.5 × 10^−4^ (maximum 7.4 × 10^−4^; Pearson r = 1.000; median 34 iterations). The finite-difference gain along the estimated v~1~ equals σ~1~ to within 0.1% for perturbation scales between 10^−4^ and 10^−2^ and exceeds the mean gain along 20 random unit directions by 19–20 times at layer 0, 2.2–2.8 at layer 12 and 14–19 at layer 23 (means over inputs, at T = 4 and 8); below 10^−5^ the differences are dominated by float32 rounding. Read from the same materialised Jacobian, the full-scan σ~1~ exceeds the largest one-step cross-position gain ‖∂y~t+1~/∂x~t~‖ by 2.9–6.2 times at layer 0, 1.9–4.3 at layer 12 and 18–30 at layer 23 (T = 8, five inputs), and lies within 1–16% of the largest same-position (token-local) gain over positions, so the leading direction of the full-scan map at these lengths is a same-position perturbation. The original run reported the last comparison as 28–63× against an eight-probe finite-difference estimate of the one-step gain; the rerun reproduces that estimate (26–74×, means 30–43 by layer) and shows it to be an artefact of the probes, which return a small fraction of a norm (Fig. 1e); the exact values replace it (Extended Data Fig. 1). The full-scan operator is a different map from the token-local operator the survey reports for Mamba; the token-local operator is validated exactly on Mamba-130M above.

### Comparison with the Aubry et al. implementation

The released code of Aubry et al.{{aubry}} (github.com/sugolov/coupling) computes, for one token position, the full d × d Jacobian of the block output with respect to the block input by vectorised reverse-mode products over the rows of the identity, subtracts the identity for the branch Jacobian, and takes singular values by full decomposition. It is the same token-local object as ours. We ran their code and ours on GPT-2 124M and Pythia-410M with five WikiText-103 validation inputs of 128 tokens at the last position, in float32 and bfloat16, on one RTX PRO 6000 (Blackwell). Two versions of their pipeline were run: as released, which takes block boundaries from the model's returned hidden states, and on the block's own input and output tensors. In float32 the block σ~1~ agreed with the power iteration to a median relative difference of 5 × 10^−5^ (GPT-2) and 8 × 10^−5^ (Pythia-410M), maximum 3 × 10^−4^, and the branch σ~1~(J − I) to 5–8 × 10^−5^; Spearman and Pearson correlations over depth were 1.000, and R~ex0~ agreed to three decimals (0.705 against 0.705; 0.854 against 0.854). In bfloat16 the median difference was 0.2% and the maximum 0.8% (GPT-2) and 5% (Pythia-410M, Spearman 0.997), with the power iteration reaching its 50-iteration cap, which is the precision effect reported above. The released version differs at the last block only, because the final entry of the returned hidden states carries the final layer normalisation: last-block σ~1~ 8.35 against 5.06 in Pythia-410M and 6.38 against 12.11 in GPT-2, moving R~ex0~ from 0.854 to 0.811 and from 0.705 to 0.806, the latter across the classification threshold. Cost in float32, five inputs: the released pipeline took 0.19 s per block on Pythia-410M and 0.12 s on GPT-2 with peak memory 1.1 GB and 0.8 GB above the resident model; the power iteration took 0.11 s and 0.07 s per block quantity (block and branch together 27 s and 8 s against 23 s and 7 s) with 0.1 GB above resident. The materialised approach scales with d in products and with d³ in the decomposition; the power iteration converged in a median of 11–18 steps independent of d. The last-token profile correlated with our eight-position profile at Spearman 0.93 (Pythia-410M) and 0.97 (GPT-2), with R~ex0~ 0.854 against 0.840 and 0.705 against 0.713.

### Protocol dependence

Eight models (GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, Pythia-6.9B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mamba-130M, ConvNeXt-tiny and ConvNeXt-base), plus five further decoders for the text protocols, were profiled under four probe protocols with the same estimator and settings: the canonical protocol (token in natural context, natural inputs); the same with uniformly random token ids or pixels; the isolated protocol, in which the block receives the single probed position and nothing else; and the whole-sequence protocol, in which the Jacobian is that of the full block map on all T positions at once and σ~1~ is taken over the Td-dimensional input. Convolutional models have no isolated protocol. The spectral radius ρ(J) was estimated under the canonical protocol by power iteration on J itself, reporting the median of the last five iterates of ‖Jv‖ to absorb the oscillation of complex-conjugate eigenpairs, on eight models (GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, Pythia-6.9B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mamba-130M, ConvNeXt-tiny and ConvNeXt-base), with the same inputs, positions, restarts and iteration count as the canonical run, and summarised by the same R~ex0~ and late-uptick rule. Replacing the residual block by its branch (J − I) changed σ~1~ by at most 0.52 over the 210 exact decompositions and R~ex0~ by at most 0.02 across the seven decoders; Weyl's inequality bounds the difference in σ~1~ by 1 but says nothing about the profile shape, so the agreement is empirical.

### Precision protocol

Forward-mode automatic differentiation is not reliable in half precision, so reduced-precision runs replace the forward product by a finite difference, Jv ≈ (f(x + εv) − f(x))/ε with ε relative to ‖x‖, and keep the adjoint step J^T^w by reverse-mode autograd through the half-precision forward; the update is otherwise the same as in float32, and the matched float32 control uses the same finite-difference forward product at the same ε. Seven models of 124M–1.5B parameters (GPT-2 124M and XL, Pythia-160M/410M/1B/1.4B, Qwen2.5-1.5B) were profiled in float16, bfloat16 and float32 at ε ∈ {10^−3^, 10^−2^, 10^−1^}, and Pythia-2.8B, 6.9B and 12B in float16 and float32 at ε = 10^−3^. In float32 at ε = 10^−3^ the finite-difference and automatic-differentiation estimates agree to a median of 0.3% per block (maximum 4% at any block except one block of Pythia-1.4B at 24%) and R~ex0~ agrees to within 0.05; at ε = 10^−2^ and 10^−1^ the float32 finite difference departs from the Jacobian (GPT-2 124M R~ex0~ 0.67 and 0.65 against 0.71; Pythia-1.4B 0.64 and 0.89 against 0.70), which bounds the step at which any finite-difference profile can be read as σ~1~. Non-finite outputs are recorded per block rather than stopping the run, and the maximum absolute hidden-state value per block on the captured forward pass is stored with each result, so an overflowing forward is visible as such. These results characterise the eager-attention, float32-accumulation-free forward of the Hugging Face implementations under the settings stated, not reduced-precision inference in general; other kernels, accumulation choices or step sizes were not tested. The operational-precision test measured directional gain ‖F(x + εv) − F(x)‖/‖εv‖ along the float32 v~1~ and along 30 random unit directions at 16 scales under five regimes (float32; autocast float16 with float32 accumulation; bfloat16; float16 weights with float32 forward; full float16) on Mamba-130M at layers 0, 12 and 23. The gain-profile test injected 50 random unit perturbations per layer at ε = 10^−2^ over ten natural-language inputs under torch.no_grad() in float32 and float16 on GPT-2 124M and Qwen2.5-0.5B, and compared per-layer profiles by Spearman rank correlation. Where the float16 forward pass was finite, the float16 profile agreed with float32 to within 0.01 in R~ex0~; the six of seven Pythia models and Qwen2.5-1.5B in which it was non-finite from some block onward are shaded in Extended Data Fig. 2e (the three largest) and marked in Extended Data Fig. 2g.

### Middle-to-edge gain ratio and interior valley

R = mean(σ~1~ over the middle third of blocks)/mean(σ~1~ over the two edge thirds), with blocks 1 to L − 1 divided into thirds and any remainder assigned to the middle. R~ex0~ excludes block 0 because its σ~1~ (35–195 in the Pythia, GPT-2 and Qwen decoders, against 2.3–19 for every other block) dominates the edge mean. A profile has an interior valley if R~ex0~ < 0.80 and both edge thirds exceed the middle third (early/middle > 1 and late/middle > 1). The two-arm condition excludes monotone profiles in either direction; an earlier rule that required only the late third to exceed the middle admitted rising profiles whose large late third alone pulled the pooled-edge ratio below 0.80, and under it Pythia-70M (early/middle 0.96) and Pythia-160M (0.75) were counted, giving 16 rather than 14 positives (Extended Data Table 1 reports both). The continuous ratio is primary and the valley classification secondary; in the main text R~ex0~ is called the middle-to-edge gain ratio. A 95% confidence interval on R~ex0~ was obtained by resampling the five inputs as clusters, each carrying its eight positions across all blocks, 2,000 resamples; the median half-width (0.034) is the same as under resampling of the 40 samples individually (0.033), and eight models' intervals straddle 0.80 (Pythia-160M, 1B, 2.8B and 6.9B, Phi-2, DINOv2-base, ViT-base and, with an interval of 0.12–0.82 about a ratio of 0.22, the Flan-T5-large encoder). Five clusters is a small number for a percentile bootstrap. The 11 models of Figs 2, 3 and 5 were profiled again with 30 inputs (survey_sigma1_v2 unchanged except for the number of inputs; e3_analysis.py): the ratio moved by at most 0.08 (median 0.02), no two-arm classification changed, and the 30-input value lay outside the five-input interval in three models (Pythia-1.4B, ConvNeXt-base and Mamba-130M), so five-input intervals understate the sampling spread. Six disjoint five-input subsets of the 30 inputs spread by up to 0.17 (ViT-base). With the 30-input values substituted, the census association is 0.04 (95% CI −0.26 to 0.34) and the decoder-only association is unchanged. The Hartigan dip test on the 45 values gives p = 0.71, and sweeping the threshold from 0.60 to 0.95 gives 6 to 22 interior-valley models (14 at 0.80). The controlled experiments are summarised by the same R~ex0~ and two-arm rule; the spectral contrast and quadratic fit of the earlier classifications are retained in the result files (the controlled-model scripts define C over thirds of all 12 blocks normalised by the mean σ~1~; the survey defines it over three-block edges and a three-block centre normalised by the edge mean) but are not used in the text.

### Metric comparison

Centred kernel alignment{{kornblith}}, effective rank and the Frobenius norm were computed per block on Pythia-70M, 160M, 410M, 1.4B and 6.9B under the canonical protocol, on the same inputs and probe positions as σ~1~, and summarised with the same middle-to-edge ratio (Extended Data Fig. 3). Centred kernel alignment is the linear form, taken between each block's input representation and its output over the whole sequence with features centred. Effective rank is exp(−Σ p~i~ log p~i~) with p~i~ the normalised singular values of the centred block input. ‖J‖~F~ is of the same token-local Jacobian as σ~1~, estimated by 64 Hutchinson probes per position, using E‖Jv‖² = ‖J‖~F~² for v ~ N(0, I~d~); σ~1~ for the ratio is taken from the survey run rather than recomputed (Extended Data Fig. 3).

### Mask × loss design

Setup 1: 12-layer pre-norm transformer{{vaswani}} (d = 512, 8 heads, untied prediction head, 89.4M parameters), WikiText-103, 10,000 steps, AdamW, five seeds per condition. The conditions are defined by an attention mask and a loss. Autoregressive: causal mask (position i attends to j ≤ i), next-token cross-entropy at every position. Causal masked-token: the same causal mask; 15% of positions are selected, of which 80% are replaced by the end-of-text token, 10% by a random token and 10% left unchanged, and the loss is cross-entropy on the original token at the selected positions only. Masked language modelling: no attention mask (every position attends to every other), the same corruption and loss. Prefix language modelling: the first 64 positions attend to all 128 positions and the last 64 attend causally, with next-token loss on the last 64 only; because the prefix positions attend to the suffix, suffix predictions can access later suffix tokens through prefix representations from the second block onward, so this condition is not a leakage-free prefix model and is reported apart from the main comparison (Extended Data Fig. 5c). The bidirectional next-token cell is degenerate (the target is visible) and was not run. The design therefore compares loss at fixed causal mask (autoregressive against causal masked-token) and mask at fixed masked-token loss (causal against bidirectional), and does not estimate a mask × loss interaction. The experiment was re-run in September 2026 with a leakage-free prefix mask (prefix positions attend within the prefix only), 500 warm-up steps, the tokenised WikiText-103 training set of the grid experiments (899,728 sequences, 256 held out), and profiling through survey_sigma1_v2's estimator with the condition's own mask at profile time (five validation inputs, eight positions, four restarts, 50 iterations); the leakage-free prefix condition reached a validation loss of 4.81 against 0.42–0.58 for the leaking mask. The values in the text are from this run; the March run is superseded (Supplementary Note 5). Setup 2: 8-layer model (d = 768, 12 heads, 95.5M parameters), C4, 12,000 steps, autoregressive and masked conditions × three seeds.

### Destroyed-data experiment

Matched 12-layer pre-norm transformers (d = 512, 8 heads, feed-forward width 2,048, untied 50,257-token prediction head, 89.4M parameters) were trained for 10,000 steps at sequence length 128, batch size 16, AdamW, learning rate 3 × 10^−4^, weight decay 0.01, 500 warm-up steps and gradient clipping at 1.0 under four conditions: causal training on WikiText-103; the same text with tokens permuted once within each sequence (unigram statistics preserved, order destroyed); real text with each target replaced by a uniformly random token resampled every batch; and bidirectional masked training on real text with the corruption of the mask × loss design. ### Condition switching

Models were trained for 5,000 steps under one condition and 5,000 under another, in both directions between token-shuffled and real text, three seeds per direction, with optimiser state and learning rate preserved across the switch. Geometry was profiled at steps 1,000, 3,000, 5,000 and 10,000 and compared with the pure conditions at 10,000 steps. ### Perturbation interventions

The perturbation intervention rotates the branch outputs of a block. For a selected block with residual update h~l+1~ = h~l~ + A~l~(h~l~) + F~l~(h~l~ + A~l~(h~l~)), the attention output A~l~ and the feed-forward output F~l~ are each multiplied, before the residual add, by a random orthogonal matrix Q drawn from the Haar measure on O(d) (QR decomposition of a Gaussian matrix, Q multiplied by the sign of diag(R)), one matrix per branch. This preserves the norm of each branch output and disrupts the learned alignment between the branch and the residual stream; it does not in general preserve the norm of the resulting residual state, because it changes the angle between branch and skip contributions, so equal rotation dose is not equal perturbation magnitude across blocks, and the per-block magnitude is reported. The same intervention is used in the controlled experiments, the k-gram sweep and the pretrained census. Sensitivity is ΔL = L~perturbed~ − L~clean~ in nats. Branch rotation disrupts the learned relationship between a branch output and the residual stream to which it is added; its consequence measures the network's sensitivity to that disruption, not the indispensability of the block's computation, and although Q is invertible on the branch vector, the resulting residual state is not an invertible image of the clean one, so no information-preservation claim is made for the full state. Identity skipping, which replaces a block's output by its input, removes the computation while preserving alignment and is the more direct test of necessity; it is reported alongside rotation in the k-gram sweep. Whether the disruption can be compensated is tested separately by the downstream-only adaptation experiment (Supplementary Note 4). The controlled models have an embedding layer followed by 12 blocks; regions are early (blocks 0–3), interior or waist (4–7) and late (8–11), and the embedding is rotated and reported separately. An interpolation parameter with QR re-orthogonalisation gave low-dose variants at 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75. Construct validity against resample, mean and zero ablation, and the downstream-only adaptation test (500 fine-tuning steps of the blocks after a rotated block 5, with a no-rotation control), are in Supplementary Note 4. Branch rotation was compared block by block with two alternatives, whole-block rotation Q[h~l~ + F~l~(h~l~)] of the residual state itself and identity skipping, on GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, Pythia-6.9B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mamba-130M, ConvNeXt-tiny and ConvNeXt-base with the census evaluation sets (30 × 16 × 128 tokens; 480 CIFAR-100 images for the ConvNeXts, read out as KL to the clean logits), recording for each block ΔL with a bootstrap interval and the perturbation magnitude ‖h′~l+1~ − h~l+1~‖/‖h~l+1~‖ at the block output on the first batch. A plumbing control that rotates block l's output by Q and block l + 1's input by Q^T^ reproduced the clean loss to within 5 × 10^−6^ nats in every model (it is skipped at the three ConvNeXt stage boundaries, where the width changes). ### Dependency-order sweep

Thirty transformers were trained on k-gram Markov sources fitted to the 20,000-sequence WikiText-103 corpus of the routing experiment (vocabulary 50,257; for each context of k − 1 tokens the 200 most frequent successors with their empirical probabilities, backing off to shorter contexts and finally to the unigram distribution) with k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8}, five seeds per value, a fixed corpus of 20,000 generated sequences of length 128 per k and seed, and the same 63.6M-parameter architecture as the routing experiment (AdamW, learning rate 3 × 10^−4^, weight decay 0.01, 500 warm-up steps then cosine decay, 10,000 steps). The models were retrained in September 2026 and profiled with the canonical estimator on five held-out sequences from their own source and five WikiText-103 sequences at initialisation, step 5,000 and step 10,000. Sensitivity was measured on 800 held-out sequences from the same source: branch rotation of the early (0–3), waist (4–7) and late (8–11) blocks at doses 0.25, 0.5 and 1, identity skipping of the same regions, per-block rotation and skipping, and early-exit loss. The mixed-effects model regresses waist ΔL on log k with a random intercept per k. Conditional entropy of each source was computed from the empirical tables. The entropy-perturbation control trained 60 further models in a crossed design (k ∈ {2, 5, 8} × uniform-noise mixing ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9} × five seeds). Early-exit probes applied the final layer normalisation and tied prediction head at each block and reported the first block whose loss fell within 1.1× the final loss and the trapezoidal area under the loss-normalised depth curve. Per-layer scrambling maps were position-corrected by dividing each layer's disruption by the number of downstream layers it can corrupt; a fixed-downstream-depth correction is in Supplementary Note 3. Windowed scrambling used independent rotations per layer in contiguous windows of width 2, 3 and 4 over the 12 blocks; the additivity ratio is observed window disruption divided by the sum of single-layer disruptions, with k = 1 excluded a priori because its body disruption was below 0.01 nats. A ratio near one indicates approximate additivity under this intervention and readout; it does not by itself distinguish distributed from serial organisation, since saturation of the loss under large perturbations would also produce sub-additive values, and no architectural conclusion is drawn from it. Layer-skip sensitivity replaced each block's output with its input and reported the NLL increase; the skip-to-scramble ratio is the mean body-layer skip NLL divided by the mean body-layer scrambling NLL per k.

### One-hop synthetic sources

Sixty 12-layer transformers (d = 512, 42M parameters over a 4,096-token vocabulary) were trained for 10,000 steps (batch 16, sequence 128, AdamW, learning rate 3 × 10^−4^) on synthetic sources with fresh data drawn every batch. In the lag family the token at position t equals the token at t − k with probability 0.8 and a uniform token otherwise; in the pair family it equals, with the same probability, the sum modulo 4,096 of the tokens at t − 1 and t − k; k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32}, five seeds each. Every source has conditional entropy 2.163 nats and a uniform-prediction baseline of 8.318 nats. Each model was profiled with the canonical estimator and probed by identity skipping of each block, whole-block rotation by region at doses 0.25, 0.5 and 1 (saturating at dose 1 as in the pretrained models, and not used), and early-exit loss at each block, on 50 held-out batches. In the lag family skipping block 0 or 1 costs 1–4.6 nats and any of blocks 2–11 at most 0.05 nats at every lag, so the first one or two blocks solve the task; R~ex0~ averages 0.84–1.01 per lag (0.75–1.21 across the 30 runs) and no run meets the two-arm rule. The pair family reached the ceiling in every run and is reported only as a failure to learn.

### Singular-direction perturbation and residual-gain stress

For each k-gram model the top five right singular vectors of the block-5 Jacobian taken over the whole 128-token input (all positions, causal mask) were obtained by randomised singular value decomposition and applied as input perturbations ±ε·v with ε = 0.1 × mean activation norm, averaged over sign, against 20 norm-matched random directions and a dimensionality-matched random-subspace control. Residual-gain stress rescaled the residual branch at evaluation, h~l+1~ = h~l~ + α·F~l~(h~l~), at α ∈ {0.75, 1.0, 1.25} for each region r, with curvature S~r~ = (L(1.25, r) + L(0.75, r) − 2L(1.0))/0.0625.

**Entropy-matched Markov family.** Thirty models with the k-gram architecture, optimiser, schedule and measurements were trained on random back-off Markov sources over a vocabulary of V = 8 tokens: the transition logits of a context are the sum over r = 1..m of independent standard-normal tables indexed by the last r tokens, so every order contributes and the order-m table is needed to reach the floor, and the exact conditional entropy H(X~t~ | last m) under the stationary law is set to 1.000 nats by a temperature found by bisection, with low-order tables redrawn until the marginal entropy lies within 0.1 nats of ln 8 (mutual information 0.99–1.07 nats at every order). **Interpretation of σ~1~ in a residual block.** For h~l+1~ = h~l~ + f~l~(h~l~) the Jacobian is J = I + Df~l~, and σ~1~(J) measures the amplification of an input change, not the magnitude of the branch output. A block that acts as an orthogonal rotation, h~l+1~ = Qh~l~ = h~l~ + (Q − I)h~l~, has σ~1~ = 1 with an active branch, so a value near one does not indicate an inactive branch; the branch-rotation and skipping interventions, not σ~1~, measure what the branch contributes.

**Direction specificity at the same position.** The same-token Jacobian describes the change at the perturbed position only, whereas the all-positions divergence also collects the changes the block passes to later positions, so the squared gain ratio is a complete isotropic baseline only for the same-position readout under a causal mask, where later positions cannot feed back. Over blocks 1–10 of the 45 causal models (450 pairs) the same-position divergence along v~1~ is 9.7 times that along a random direction (median), against a squared gain ratio of 3.0, an excess of 3.0 (5th to 95th percentile 1.0–14.6; above 1 in 427 of 450 pairs; medians by group real text 3.3, shuffled 7.3, random labels 1.0, k = 1 11.2, k = 2–8 2.6–2.9); the all-positions readout gives 11.3 and 3.7 on the same 45 models, and 11 and 3.7 over all 50 models with the five bidirectional ones included (500 pairs; Extended Data Fig. 7f).

### Bracket-nesting probes

Four 12-layer transformers (seeds 0–3) were trained for 10,000 steps on a Dyck language with eight bracket types and 48 filler tokens (64-token vocabulary; nesting depth up to 20; sequences of 128 tokens). Linear probes for nesting depth and top-of-stack identity were fitted at every layer and the participation ratio of the layerwise representation was computed. The per-block cost was measured by rotating the residual stream at the input of one block at a time by a random orthogonal matrix (dose 1); this is a whole-stream rotation, not the branch rotation of the main experiments.

### Pretrained geometry–sensitivity census

Geometry for the census is the survey R~ex0~ and two-arm flag of each model. Sensitivity was measured by per-layer scrambling of all 45 surveyed models: a Haar-random orthogonal rotation of each sublayer's branch output before its residual add, applied one block at a time at dose 1.0, with the loss increase ΔL measured on 30 batches (16 sequences or images, four utterances) and a 200-sample bootstrap interval over batches. The branch outputs are the attention and feed-forward outputs for the decoders, the masked encoders, ViT, DINOv2, T5 and the audio encoders (for the post-normalisation architectures BERT, RoBERTa and wav2vec2, the linear projection before the residual add and normalisation), the SSM mixer for Mamba, the second pointwise convolution for ConvNeXt and the token-mixing and channel-mixing outputs for MLP-Mixer. The readout is each model's own objective on natural inputs: next-token cross-entropy on WikiText-103 validation sequences for the decoders and Mamba; masked-token cross-entropy on the same sequences (15% masking, 80/10/10, masks fixed) for BERT and RoBERTa; span-corruption cross-entropy (15% noise, mean span 3, spans fixed; encoder scrambled, decoder untouched) for the T5 encoders; connectionist temporal classification loss against the reference transcript on LibriSpeech test-clean (first 10 s of 120 utterances) for wav2vec2; teacher-forced decoder cross-entropy on the reference transcript for the Whisper encoder (both on utterances of at most 10 s, uncropped, with their full transcripts; an earlier run that cropped longer utterances is superseded, Supplementary Note 5); and KL(clean ‖ scrambled) over the ImageNet-1k classification head on CIFAR-100 test images for ConvNeXt, ViT, DINOv2 (the backbone with its released ImageNet linear head) and MLP-Mixer, with the self-label cross-entropy recorded alongside; for these models the readout is a change in the output distribution, not a measured change in classification accuracy. Every model re-run in September also records KL(clean ‖ scrambled) over its own output distribution. Rotating the branch output rather than the block output leaves the residual stream itself unrotated; an earlier whole-block rotation of Mamba and ConvNeXt, which rotates the residual stream, gave baselines of 31–42 nats and was discarded. A May 2026 run that supplied the masked-encoder, T5, ViT, DINOv2, MLP-Mixer and audio files had read those models out by the variance of their final hidden state on random inputs and, for BERT, RoBERTa, T5 and the ViT feed-forward, had hooked module outputs that already include the residual add; those 14 models were re-run on 20 September 2026 with the hooks and readouts above (Extended Data Table 3), and the decoder, Mamba and ConvNeXt files, whose hooks and readouts were already as described, were kept. Regional means were taken over thirds of blocks 1 to L − 2; the first and last block are reported separately because block 0 contributes 3.6–10.1 nats in the 23 decoders and the last block up to 23 nats. Relative interior sensitivity is the waist mean divided by the larger edge-third mean (the waist-to-edge ratio). Three exclusion criteria were applied, and a model failing any is excluded from the census statistic but shown in Extended Data Fig. 6: the mean ΔL over all interior blocks (1 to L − 2, not the middle third alone) must be non-negative within 0.01 nats, because a rotation that lowers the task loss cannot be read as a dependence measurement under this design; the waist-to-edge ratio must be defined, which fails when no edge third is positive; and a cross-entropy readout must lie below the uniform-prediction baseline ln V of its vocabulary, the cross-entropy of uniform predictions. No model fails the first two (Flan-T5-large has a negative middle-third mean, −0.22 nats, but an interior mean of +0.50). Flan-T5-large fails the third, the only criterion it fails: its span-corruption loss on the clean model is 13.6 nats against a baseline of 10.4, the instruction-tuned model no longer performing the denoising task at its pretraining operating point, and its per-block values are reported without being counted. **Downstream linearised response.** For a probe (block l, position t) and a unit direction u at the block input, the same-position divergence is, to second order, D~KL~ ≈ ½ε^2^‖h~l,t~‖^2^ g^T^Fg with g = Au, where A is the Jacobian of the position-t logits with respect to the block-l input at t (the whole downstream stack, block l included) and F = diag(p) − pp^T^ is the Fisher metric of the output distribution; g is one forward-mode product through the model. The product factorises into an amplification ‖g‖^2^, the squared growth of the unit displacement from the block-l input to the position-t logits, and an output alignment g^T^Fg/‖g‖^2^. Adding a constant to every logit changes ‖g‖ but not g^T^Fg, because F annihilates the constant vector, so the split into amplification and alignment is specific to the logit coordinates; their product, which gives the divergence, is not. The after-block factor defined below includes the final normalisation and the output head as well as the later blocks. Because A includes block l, ‖g‖^2^ is total input-to-logit amplification; dividing it by the squared local gain along the same direction (σ~1~ for v~1~, and the matched assay's own random-direction gain at ε = 0.01 for the random class, the two runs having drawn the same directions from the same generator, which the random-direction divergences confirm to within 1.1%) leaves the amplification contributed by the blocks after block l, so the same-position consequence ratio factorises as (σ~1~/g~rand~)^2^ × after-block amplification × alignment (linresp_afterblock.py). On the eight checkpoints of the sampling-check subset (five inputs × four positions, v~1~ and four random directions per probe; linearised_response.py, 21 Sept 2026; linresp_analysis.py) the prediction reproduced the measured same-position divergence to a median ratio of 1.000 at ε = 0.01, 0.992–1.003 at ε = 0.03 and 0.93–1.04 at ε = 0.1. Over blocks 1–10, the block-to-block variance of log consequence per unit displacement was carried by amplification (share 0.43–1.04) rather than alignment (0.01–0.49; in the k = 8 models both terms are large and anticorrelated), and σ~1~ tracked total amplification in the training-condition models (Spearman 0.50–0.92) but not in the k-gram models (−0.67 to 0.54). The excess consequence of v~1~ over a random direction was amplification, not alignment. Taking geometric means over probes and blocks 1–10, with the arithmetic mean over the four random directions of each probe (the convention of the measured random-direction divergence), total amplification is 8.5–11.0 times a random direction on real text, shuffled text and k = 8 and 29.8–31.2 at k = 1, against an alignment ratio of 0.96–1.62 and 0.47–0.63 at k = 1, where v~1~ is less aligned with the output metric than a random direction and far more amplified. The three factors reconstruct the measured same-position ratio to 0.92–0.99 of its value. Of the total amplification the squared local gain contributes 2.1–5.4 (1.5 at k = 1) and the blocks after the tested one contribute 2.0–4.2 (19.6–21.1 at k = 1), the after-block factor exceeding one in 83–100% of probes in every model, so the leading local direction is favoured both at the tested block and beyond it. Within a model σ~1~ does not rank the after-block factor across blocks (Spearman −0.77 to 0.19), so the two are separate quantities. Alignment is small in absolute terms (g^T^Fg/‖g‖^2^ = 4 × 10^−6^ to 5 × 10^−5^), since most of a logit change falls on low-probability tokens.

### Matched assay in pretrained decoders

GPT-2 124M, Pythia-410M, Pythia-1.4B, Llama-3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B (a beginning-of-sequence token prepended for Gemma-2-2B, as in the census) were probed with the matched assay and the linearised response in one run (matched_pretrained.py; float32, eager attention, TF32 disabled, one NVIDIA A100 40 GB, 8–198 min per model). Inputs were 20 WikiText-103 validation sequences of 128 tokens spread evenly through the split, at positions t ∈ {8, 32, 56, 80, 104, 120}, for every block. σ~1~ and v~1~ came from the *J*^T^*J* power iteration on the survey's in-context token-local map (two restarts, 50 iterations, tolerance 10^−6^). The block input at t was displaced by ±ε‖h~l,t~‖u with u = v~1~ or one of eight random unit vectors drawn once per input and position, ε ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3}, signs averaged, with the four readouts of the controlled assay. The linearised response was computed on the first five inputs for v~1~ and the same eight random directions. Within-model correlations are over blocks 1 to L − 2, with 95% intervals from 500 resamples of whole inputs; pooled regressions express depth as block/(L − 1) because L differs between models, with standard errors clustered on the six models; out-of-model prediction holds out one model or one family, the two Pythia models forming one family (matched_pretrained_analysis.py, e1_extra.py). The four predictor sets of the controlled analysis were compared on the same folds with depth as block/(L − 1) (predictor_check.py), and each fold's R^2^ was given a 95% interval from 500 resamples of the held-out model's inputs with the training fit held fixed (family_transfer.py): pooled over the family folds the sets give 0.02 (−0.08 to 0.08), 0.04, −0.04 and 0.32 (0.23 to 0.38), and over the model folds 0.07, 0.06, 0.07 and 0.24 (0.14 to 0.30). ### Statistics

The primary test for the dependency-order effect used the six k-group means as inferential units and an exact permutation test over the 720 orderings of six groups, with the directional hypothesis (ΔL~waist~ increasing in k) specified before the experiment; the observed ordering is the one extreme ordering, p = 1/720 (one-sided). The same test over the five learned orders of the entropy-matched family gives p = 1/120. A mixed-effects model ΔL~ij~ = β~0~ + β~1~ log k~i~ + u~i~ + ε~ij~, with a random intercept u~i~ for each k level and seeds j as replicates within level, was fitted as a co-primary confirmation; with one outcome per seed the seed carries no random effect of its own, and with six levels the variance of u is weakly identified, so the permutation test is primary; the fitted slope is 0.42 ± 0.06 nats per unit log k (z = 6.6). In the entropy-perturbation control the within-k effect of noise was tested by one-way ANOVA at each k (p ≥ 0.052 at every k) and the confidence-adjusted association by partial Spearman correlation, which was inconclusive because confidence and order share most of their rank variance (partial ρ 0.33–0.37, p 0.05–0.09); the cell means and the ANOVA are in Supplementary Note 2; an absence of a detected effect is not a demonstration of equivalence. Other comparisons report mean ± s.d. across seeds, Spearman or Pearson correlations as stated, Welch's t-test for the residual-gain contrast and a paired t-test across the nine k-gram models for singular-direction perturbation.
