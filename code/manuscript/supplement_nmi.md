# Supplementary Notes

**Supplementary Note 1: Bracket-nesting probes and compact state representation**

*Provenance: May 2026 run of four models; the per-seed result files are retained (exp2a_bracket_seed0–3.json) and the checkpoints are not. The values below are recomputed from those files. Shown in Extended Data Fig. 8a.*

This note reports the bracket-nesting probe experiment of Extended Data Fig. 8a. The experiment tests whether a task-relevant state variable, nesting depth in a bracket language, is decodable from early layers, and whether scrambling loss persists across layers even after the state has converged.

**Design.** Four 12-layer transformers (seeds 0–3) were trained for 10,000 steps on a bracket-nesting language: a Dyck language with eight bracket types and 48 filler tokens (64-token vocabulary), nesting depth up to 20, sequences of 128 tokens. At each layer, two linear probes were trained: (1) a depth-regression probe predicting the current nesting depth (R² metric), and (2) a top-of-stack classification probe predicting the identity of the most recent unmatched bracket (accuracy metric). The per-block cost was measured by rotating the residual stream at the input of one block at a time by a random orthogonal matrix (dose 1); this is a whole-stream rotation, not the branch rotation of the main text. Participation ratio (PR) of the layer-wise representation was also computed as a measure of effective dimensionality.

**Results.** Values are means over the four seeds. A linear probe for nesting depth reached R² = 0.98 at the output of block 0 (0.06 at the embedding) and stayed at 0.98–0.99 through block 11 (at least 0.97 in every seed). The top-of-stack probe reached 0.85 accuracy after block 1 and 1.00 from block 2 onward. Per-block rotation cost fell from 2.38 nats at block 0 to 0.87 nats at block 11 and was no lower at any block. The participation ratio of the representation fell from 45 at the embedding to 7–15 across the blocks.

**Interpretation.** The probe results show that task-relevant state (nesting depth, top-of-stack identity) is fully decodable from a compact subspace by block 1 or 2. The persistent scrambling ΔL across all subsequent layers cannot therefore reflect the progressive construction of this state. Instead, it reflects the cost of reorienting the compact subspace: downstream layers depend on the specific directional basis in which the state is encoded, and rotating that basis (even norm-preservingly) produces loss because the downstream readout is calibrated to a particular coordinate system. This supports the interpretation that scrambling measures alignment-disruption sensitivity rather than computational necessity (Supplementary Note 4), and that the interior blocks propagate an already-formed state in a direction-sensitive manner rather than progressively computing it.

**Supplementary Note 2: Entropy, confidence and robustness controls**

*Provenance note (September 2026): the analyses in this note are the May 2026 confidence and entropy controls. Retained files, all in the results manifest: the 30 per-model files and the log of the confidence-normalised run (d3e_confidence/, d3e_confidence_results.txt), the per-model values of the covariate re-run (s6_covariates/s6_combined.json) and the generator entropy report (d3b_entropy_report.json). The Design A per-model files were not retained (see below). The September main text does not rest on this note.*

**Alternative confidence covariates.** The confidence-normalised analysis (d3e; 30 models, five per k) reported that the dependency-order effect on middle-block sensitivity persists after controlling for model confidence using partial Spearman correlation with the original max-probability covariate. To test whether alternative confidence measures give the same result, the partial-Spearman analysis was repeated with two additional covariates: predictive entropy H~pred~ (the Shannon entropy of the model's output distribution) and post-temperature top-1 probability (the maximum softmax probability after applying temperature T = 1.5).

All 30 k-gram models were retrained from scratch with the corrected backoff-chain generator to ensure that k-gram sequences for k ≥ 3 correctly condition on the full preceding context. In this re-run the middle-block cost is the loss increase when the residual stream at the input of blocks 4–7 is rotated, all four blocks together, by one random orthogonal matrix per block (five draws, dose 1; the May 2026 waist protocol), so it is larger than the per-block branch-rotation costs of the main text. The raw Spearman correlation between ΔL~waist~ and k was ρ = 0.982 (p < 10^−21^), confirming the monotonic dependency-order effect.

All three confidence covariates correlated very strongly with k: ρ(max_prob, k) = 0.987, ρ(H~pred~, k) = −0.987, ρ(top1_post_temp, k) = 0.987. After partialling out each covariate, the residual correlation between ΔL~waist~ and k dropped substantially: partial ρ | max_prob = 0.325 (p = 0.085), partial ρ | H~pred~ = 0.358 (p = 0.056), partial ρ | top1_post_temp = 0.368 (p = 0.049).

These results show that dependency order and model confidence are deeply entangled in the k-gram design (ρ > 0.98 between all confidence measures and k). Partial correlation cannot cleanly separate two variables that share 97% of their rank variance. The marginal significance of the residual (p approximately 0.05-0.09) means that the partial-Spearman approach alone is inconclusive for distinguishing dependency order from confidence.

The stronger evidence against a pure confidence artefact comes from the convergent metrics of the May 2026 confidence-normalised analysis. Symmetric KL divergence between clean and scrambled output distributions increased with k even among models with similar confidence levels. Top-token flip rate and correct-token rank degradation both increased with k. These measures capture the magnitude of distributional disruption rather than relying on the level of the output distribution, and they are less susceptible to the entanglement between confidence and dependency order. The partial-Spearman analysis should therefore be interpreted as one component of a convergent-evidence strategy rather than as a standalone test.

Group means (five models per k): k = 1: ΔL~waist~ = 3.52, max_prob = 0.260, H~pred~ = 3.79, top1_T = 0.099. k = 2: 4.92, 0.371, 3.27, 0.159. k = 3: 5.53, 0.418, 3.05, 0.188. k = 4: 6.27, 0.469, 2.70, 0.223. k = 5: 6.65, 0.502, 2.51, 0.247. k = 8: 6.95, 0.531, 2.34, 0.273.

**Noise-at-fixed-k robustness test (Design A).** *Provenance note (September 2026): the per-model result files and checkpoints of this design were not retained; the cell means below are transcribed from the May 2026 notebook log and cannot be regenerated from stored outputs. The design is kept here for the record and is not cited in the main text as evidence.* The May 2026 dependency-order sweep confounded dependency order k with source entropy: higher-order k-gram sources produce lower conditional entropy (H~cond~), making their outputs more predictable. If entropy, rather than dependency depth, drives middle-block sensitivity, then two sources with the same k but different entropies should produce different depth sensitivities. Design A tests this by training models on noisy k-gram sources that vary entropy while holding k fixed.

**Protocol.** We trained 60 models in a fully crossed design: k ∈ {2, 5, 8} × noise ∈ {0.0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9} × 5 seeds. At each noise level ε the conditional distribution of every k-gram context was mixed with a uniform distribution over that context's retained successor tokens (at most 200): P = (1 − ε)·P~k-gram~ + ε·U. A context with a single recorded successor is unchanged by the mixing. All models used the same 12-layer transformer architecture, vocabulary (50,257 GPT-2 tokens), sequence length (128) and training protocol (10,000 steps, AdamW, learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁴) as the main dependency-order sweep. Middle-block sensitivity (ΔL~waist~) was the loss increase when the branch outputs of the middle blocks, identified from each model's fitted σ~1~ profile, were rotated together at dose 1.

**Results.** Middle-block sensitivity scaled with dependency order but was largely insensitive to noise within each k:

Cell means (± s.d. over five seeds) of the middle-block cost in nats at noise 0, 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9: k = 5, 1.317 ± 0.079, 1.310 ± 0.116, 1.265 ± 0.093 and 1.273 ± 0.084 (mean 1.291); k = 8, 1.472 ± 0.064, 1.467 ± 0.050, 1.427 ± 0.069 and 1.446 ± 0.068 (mean 1.453); k = 2, mean 0.493 (the cell values are not recoverable from the log).

Across all 60 models, Spearman ρ between k and ΔL~waist~ was 0.926 (p = 2.8 × 10⁻²⁶). One-way ANOVA on the noise factor within each k level showed: k = 2 F(3,16) = 3.2, p = 0.052; k = 5 F(3,16) = 0.28, p = 0.84; k = 8 F(3,16) = 0.51, p = 0.68. Noise had at most a marginal effect at k = 2 and no detectable effect at k ≥ 5.

**Diagnostic: conditional entropy.** The insensitivity at k ≥ 5 follows from the mixing rule and the sharpness of the fitted tables: at k = 5 and k = 8 the median context has a single recorded successor (mean 1.08 and 1.006 successors per context; d3b_entropy_report.json), and for such contexts the mixing changes nothing. The run's entropy diagnostic, a plug-in estimate from the successor counts of 1,000 generated sequences averaged over contexts, gave values below 0.02 nats at k = 5 with noise 0.9; at k ≥ 5 nearly every context occurs once in such a sample, so this estimate is near zero whatever the source entropy and does not measure it. Before mixing, the fitted k = 5 and k = 8 tables have conditional entropies of 0.19 and 0.011 nats, against 7.5 nats for the unigram source. At k = 2, where tables are less peaked (mean 15.5 retained successors per context), noise had a marginal effect on middle-block rotation cost (p = 0.052), consistent with entropy exerting a weak modulatory influence when the mixing can shift the data statistics.

**Interpretation.** These results support the dependency-order trend but do not fully orthogonalise entropy, confidence, and dependency order. The noise manipulation could not raise the conditional entropy at k ≥ 5, because the mixing acts within each context's retained successor set and most contexts at those orders have one. At k = 2, where noise did shift effective statistics, the effect on ΔL~waist~ was marginal (p = 0.052). The design therefore demonstrates robustness of the k–ΔL relationship to moderate noise perturbation, but it cannot rule out entropy as a partial mediator. Together with the partial-correlation analyses above, these results provide converging, but not definitive, evidence that the dependency-order relationship is not solely an artefact of the entropy-predictability confound.

**Supplementary Note 3: Position confound in per-block rotation cost**

*Provenance: May 2026 k-gram models (30 trained by d3m_position_corrected.py); retained aggregate output d3m_aggregate.json, per-model files not retained. Earlier versions of this note also reported layer-removal and unstructured-pruning comparisons on Pythia-12B; they are withdrawn (Supplementary Note 5).*

Per-block rotation cost is confounded with block position: a block near the input has more downstream blocks through which a disruption can propagate than a block near the output. Removing the blocks with the lowest per-block cost therefore selects edge blocks for their position, not for a small contribution.

**Position-corrected functional measures (D3m).** To directly quantify the position confound in the controlled k-gram models, we applied two position-correction methods to the 12-layer per-layer scrambling profiles (6 k-values × 5 seeds = 30 models). Method PC1 ("fixed downstream") reads the loss out five blocks after the rotated block (the final normalisation and head applied there; fewer blocks for the last ones), so that every block is scored over the same propagation length. Method PC2 ("normalised") divides each layer's ΔL by the number of downstream layers (L − ℓ), converting absolute disruption into disruption-per-unit-propagation-path.

The main finding, that middle-block sensitivity increases with dependency order, was robust to both corrections. Spearman ρ between k and the middle-block score (mean per-block cost over blocks 4–7; n = 30) was 0.874 (standard), 0.856 (PC1) and 0.829 (PC2), all p < 2 × 10⁻⁸ (d3m_aggregate.json). Group means of the standard score rose from 2.98 nats at k = 1 to 5.18 at k = 8, and under PC2 from 0.48 to 0.82. Position correction does not alter the conclusion that higher-order dependencies recruit more middle-block computation.

Under PC2 the magnitudes fall to about 15% of the standard values (k = 8, 5.18 to 0.82 nats), so the raw per-block costs are inflated by position. The per-block rankings of the run are not in the retained aggregate and are not reported.

Per-block rotation cost therefore mixes a block's contribution with its position, but the confound does not change the middle-block comparison across dependency orders.

**Supplementary Note 4: Intervention construct validity**

*Provenance: May 2026 runs on the k-gram models. Experiment 1 is reported from its retained aggregate values; Experiment 2 is recomputed from the retained per-seed file (d3l_alignment_adaptation/d3l_aggregate.json). Experiment 2 is shown in Extended Data Fig. 8b.*

This note reports two experiments that test whether orthogonal scrambling measures a construct distinct from existing ablation methods (Experiment 1) and whether the ΔL it produces reflects computational necessity or downstream basis sensitivity (Experiment 2). The first experiment tests whether scrambling simply replicates what resample ablation already measures; the second tests whether the loss increase after scrambling reflects disrupted basis alignment rather than disrupted computation.

**Experiment 1: Head-to-head intervention comparison**

**Design.** Ten controlled 12-layer transformers (k = 5, five seeds; k = 8, five seeds) were evaluated under four per-layer interventions: (1) orthogonal scrambling (norm-preserving random rotation), (2) resample ablation (replace activation with one from a random other context), (3) mean ablation (replace activation with the dataset-mean activation), and (4) zero ablation (replace activation with zeros). Each intervention was applied independently to each of the 12 layers, and the resulting ΔL (increase in held-out negative log-likelihood relative to clean forward pass) was recorded. For each model, the four interventions produced four layer-ranking vectors of length 12.

**Thresholds set before the run.** A mean ρ(scramble, resample) above 0.85 would indicate the same construct as resample ablation, 0.5–0.85 a correlated but distinct construct and below 0.5 a different construct.

**Results.** The mean Spearman rank correlation between orthogonal-scrambling and resample-ablation layer rankings was ρ = −0.031 (SD = 0.378) across the 10 models. Individual-model correlations ranged from −0.783 to +0.573 (Supplementary Table 1). This falls below the 0.5 threshold set before the run for a different construct. The two methods do not produce similar layer-importance rankings.

For comparison, correlations between orthogonal scrambling and mean ablation (mean ρ = 0.006, SD = 0.137) and between orthogonal scrambling and zero ablation (mean ρ = −0.009, SD = 0.213) were also near zero.

**Interpretation.** Orthogonal scrambling and resample ablation answer different questions about layer function. Resample ablation tests whether a layer's activation carries input-specific information by replacing it with an activation from a different input. Orthogonal scrambling tests whether downstream layers depend on the specific directional structure of the activation by rotating it to a random orientation while preserving its norm. A layer can carry input-specific information (high resample ΔL) without having a directionally sensitive downstream readout (low scramble ΔL), or vice versa. The near-zero correlation confirms that these are distinct constructs, not interchangeable ablation methods.

**Experiment 2: Alignment-adaptation test**

**Design.** The alignment-adaptation experiment tests whether ΔL after orthogonal scrambling reflects computational necessity of the layer or merely the sensitivity of downstream layers to basis alignment. If downstream layers can quickly learn to accept a rotated basis, then the original ΔL measured "alignment sensitivity" rather than "this layer's computation is necessary."

Five k = 8 models (seeds 0–4) were used. A fixed random orthogonal rotation was applied to the output of block 5; blocks 0–5 were frozen and blocks 6–11 were fine-tuned with AdamW (learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁴) for 500 steps, after which the cost of the rotation was measured again. In a control arm the same blocks were fine-tuned for the same number of steps without the rotation, and the cost of then applying it was measured.

**Threshold set before the run.** A reduction of the cost by more than 70% would indicate that the intervention measures alignment-disruption sensitivity rather than the necessity of the rotated block's computation.

**Results.** Adaptation reduced the cost of rotating block 5 from 5.22 ± 0.11 to 0.52 ± 0.01 nats, a reduction of 90.0 ± 0.4% (89.5–90.5% across seeds; Supplementary Table 2). Fine-tuning without the rotation left the cost at 5.27 ± 0.10 nats (−1.0 ± 0.3% change). The clean loss rose by 0.08–0.10 nats across seeds. Earlier drafts reported a two-condition pilot (5.25 to 0.09 nats) and a three-condition rerun (5.48 to 0.29 nats, 94.7%) whose result files were not retained; the values here are from the retained file.

**Interpretation.** The reduction exceeds the threshold, and the control shows that the recovery is specific to adaptation to the rotated basis. Most of the cost of a rotation therefore reflects the dependence of later blocks on the basis established upstream, not the necessity of the rotated block's computation, and the intervention is interpreted as alignment-disruption sensitivity. The recovery does not show that the full residual state was preserved.

**Combined interpretation.** The two experiments provide complementary evidence. Experiment 1 shows that orthogonal scrambling measures a different construct from existing ablation methods: its layer rankings are uncorrelated with those produced by resample, mean, or zero ablation. Experiment 2 shows that the construct being measured is primarily basis-alignment sensitivity rather than computational necessity. Together, these results establish that orthogonal scrambling captures something distinct from other ablation methods, while clarifying its interpretation: what it measures is directional coordination between layers, not computational indispensability.

**Off-manifold displacement.** Experiment 1 above showed that the layer ranking of orthogonal scrambling is uncorrelated with those of resample, mean and zero ablation (mean Spearman ρ −0.03, 0.01 and −0.01; Supplementary Table 1), consistent with different constructs. A natural follow-up question is whether these interventions also differ in how far they push activations away from the manifold of clean representations. We quantified this by computing the squared Euclidean distance from each intervened activation vector to its nearest neighbour in a cache of 1,000 randomly sampled clean activation vectors at the same layer.

Measurements were taken at the four middle blocks (layers 4-7) of the controlled k-gram models at k = 5 (n = 5 seeds) and k = 8 (n = 5 seeds). For each model, 200 input sequences were passed through the network, and the activation vectors at each middle block were recorded after applying each intervention. The nearest-neighbour distance was computed against the cached clean activations from a separate set of 1,000 sequences.

The four interventions produced a consistent ranking of off-manifold displacement. At k = 5, the mean squared distances (averaged across middle blocks and seeds) were: orthogonal 2,499 ± 104, resample 1,649 ± 48, zero 778 ± 26, mean 751 ± 24. At k = 8: orthogonal 2,055 ± 42, resample 1,427 ± 19, zero 617 ± 25, mean 600 ± 21. The ranking was identical across both k values: orthogonal displaced activations approximately 1.5x further than resample and 3.3x further than mean or zero ablation. Mean and zero ablation produced nearly identical displacement (ratio 0.97 at both k values).

Within each model, the off-manifold distance increased monotonically from layer 4 to layer 7, consistent with the expectation that deeper middle blocks accumulate more displacement from the clean manifold as the intervention propagates through the residual stream.

Two features of these results merit comment. First, the large separation between orthogonal and resample displacement is consistent with the claim that orthogonal scrambling tests a different property, directional alignment, than resample ablation, which preserves distributional statistics. Second, k = 5 models showed approximately 20% larger off-manifold distances than k = 8 models across all intervention types. This may reflect the fact that k = 8 models, having learned higher-order dependencies, develop more tightly clustered internal representations with a denser activation manifold, so that intervened vectors land closer to some clean neighbour despite the same perturbation strength.

**Supplementary Note 5: Provenance of every reported result**

This note is the single record of which run each number in the paper comes from, which earlier runs it replaces, and what can be recomputed from the deposited files. The Methods describe only the protocol in force; the history is here.

**Three classes of evidence.** (1) Regenerable. Every number, table and figure in the main text, Extended Data Figs. 2 to 7, 9 and 10, and Extended Data Tables 1 to 5 are produced from the frozen results manifest by the scripts named in Extended Data Table 3. The exact-decomposition values of Fig. 1d,e are from the 25 September 2026 rerun of the validation (validation_rerun_colab.ipynb; seven models, 210 pairs, per-pair files at full precision in the manifest). The original runs of 16 and 18 September were written to the instance disk and survive as three-decimal printed logs (cellH.log; the ConvNeXt and Mamba notebook with its output); the rerun reproduces every one of their 210 pairs to a maximum relative difference of 1.9 × 10^−4^ and a median of 2–6 × 10^−5^ per model, the precision of the logs, and its pooled maximum relative error is 3.0 × 10^−4^ where the logs gave 4.8 × 10^−4^, the difference being rounding. (2) Retained summaries. The May 2026 mechanism experiments (Extended Data Fig. 8; Supplementary Notes 1 to 4) are reported from their retained result files: per-seed files for the bracket-nesting probes (exp2a), the downstream-only adaptation (d3l_aggregate.json), the singular-direction perturbation (exp4a) and the residual-gain stress test (d3r); per-model files for the confidence controls (d3e, s6); and aggregate files for the intervention comparison (d3k), the position correction (d3m) and the off-manifold distances (d3o). Their checkpoints were not kept, so they can be re-summarised and re-plotted but not re-measured. The Design A noise experiment (Supplementary Note 2) has neither files nor checkpoints. (3) Rerun in place of an image. The Mamba full-scan validation of Extended Data Fig. 1 was first run in April 2026 (patterns_repo/experiments/mamba_sigma1_validation.py) and survives only as its figure; it was rerun on 25 September 2026 with the same design (mamba_fullscan_validation.py, one JSON per pair) and the figure regenerated from the rerun. Two things changed in the reading. The direction ratio is reported on the plateau of the finite-difference curve (10^−4^ ≤ ε ≤ 10^−2^), where the gain along v~1~ equals σ~1~; at the smallest scales float32 rounding dominates. The original right-hand panel compared the full-scan σ~1~ with an eight-probe finite-difference estimate of the one-step gain ‖∂y~t+1~/∂x~t~‖ and reported 28–63×; the rerun reproduces that estimate (26–74×) and, reading the same block exactly from the materialised Jacobian, gives 2.9–6.2× at layer 0, 1.9–4.3× at layer 12 and 18–30× at layer 23, so the earlier figure overstated the ratio by the factor by which eight random probes under-read a norm. The regenerated panel shows the exact values.

**What the earlier estimator measured.** The routing-topology, skip-coefficient and destroyed-data experiments of March 2026 and the k-gram sweep of May 2026 were first profiled by forward-mode iteration on J over the whole layer input at uniform-random token ids without the attention mask. (The March 2026 mask-by-loss run, shown for the record in Extended Data Fig. 5c, used a token-local J^T^J iteration at one position on random token ids without the attention mask; Extended Data Table 3.) That iteration converges to the spectral radius of the whole-sequence layer Jacobian, not to σ~1~ of the token-local block Jacobian, and the two differ by a median factor of 2.1 to 3.6 and by up to 18 in these models (Extended Data Fig. 2a). Every profile reported in this paper comes from the canonical estimator instead.

**Superseded results, and what replaced them.** (1) Destroyed data: the March run had reported a two-arm interior valley on real text in four of five seeds; the September re-run (d1 v2.1, same architecture, corpus, optimiser and seeds, canonical estimator on natural inputs) gives flat real-text profiles (R~ex0~ = 1.00 ± 0.03) and no interior valley in any pure condition (0 of 20). The March run had also reported the highest spectral contrast under random labels, and its iid-unigram condition, which produced non-monotonic dose responses, was not repeated. (2) Mask by loss: the March run had reported an interior valley in every causal-mask run and in none of the bidirectional runs; the September re-run (factorial_v2, 20 runs) gives declining profiles in 19 of 20 and one causal masked-token seed at the margin (R~ex0~ = 0.71). (3) The k-gram, routing-topology and skip-coefficient experiments were re-measured (reprofile_v2) and are reported from the re-runs only. (4) The census files for 14 non-decoder models from a May 2026 run with hidden-state-variance readouts were replaced by the 20 September re-run. (5) The audio census: an earlier run cropped utterances longer than 10 s while scoring against the full transcript, giving clean losses of 1.43 and 1.10 against 0.60 and 0.85 in the corrected run; the profile shape and the census association are unchanged at the reported precision. No checkpoints from any March run were retained.
